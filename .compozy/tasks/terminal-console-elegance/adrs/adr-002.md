# ADR-002: Prioritize active-run elegance as the MVP product approach

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-13

## Context

The approved idea for the **Terminal Console** elegance redesign established a broad product direction: improve visual polish, information hierarchy, and mode separation while preserving the read-first **Runtime State** boundary. The PRD process then narrowed the business priorities further.

The user selected:

- active-run monitoring as the most important MVP outcome
- the heavy daily operator as the primary persona
- removal of visual clutter and weak hierarchy as the main pain point

This creates a product trade-off. The redesign could balance startup/readiness and active-run monitoring equally, or it could optimize explicitly for the long-running operator session where the **Terminal Console** stays open as the primary working surface.

## Decision

The PRD will use an **active-run first elegance** approach for the MVP.

The MVP will focus primarily on making the **Terminal Console** a clearer, calmer, and more trustworthy live monitoring surface for heavy daily operators during active orchestration. Startup/readiness states remain in scope, but they are secondary to the main product promise: a user should be able to keep the **Terminal Console** open during long runs and understand active work without routine fallback to the **Web Dashboard**.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Balanced two-mode redesign

- **Description**: Give equal product weight to startup/readiness and active-run monitoring from the start.
- **Pros**: Symmetric product story; cleaner conceptual split; easier to explain as a general redesign.
- **Cons**: Dilutes MVP focus; reduces pressure to optimize the highest-frequency active-run workflow; spreads product attention across too many moments.
- **Why rejected**: The user prioritized active-run monitoring first, and the PRD should reflect that concentration.

### Alternative 2: Minimal visual cleanup

- **Description**: Improve spacing, grouping, and presentation while changing as little navigation behavior as possible.
- **Pros**: Fastest delivery path; low behavioral risk; easier to validate incrementally.
- **Cons**: Unlikely to solve the current meaning problem around overloaded panels and focus-only navigation; weaker product differentiation.
- **Why rejected**: The MVP needs a meaningful hierarchy improvement, not only cosmetic cleanup.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Gives the MVP a clear business target and primary success metric.
- Aligns the redesign with the most demanding operator persona.
- Increases the chance that the Terminal Console becomes the trusted default live-monitoring surface.

### Negative

- Startup/readiness improvements may feel less fully developed in the first release.
- The product story becomes less balanced across all orchestration moments.
- Some users may expect stronger blocked-startup redesign parity than the MVP delivers.

### Risks

- **Readiness underinvestment**: blocked-startup users may still feel the product is uneven.
  Mitigation: keep readiness improvements visible and functional, even if they are not the main optimization target.
- **Overfitting to power users**: the design may become dense or intimidating for lighter users.
  Mitigation: require strong hierarchy and clarity, not just higher density.
- **Weak measurement**: product success may look subjective without explicit adoption signals.
  Mitigation: anchor the PRD in monitoring retention, fallback reduction, and task comprehension metrics.

## Implementation Notes

This ADR selects the MVP product emphasis only. It does not prescribe implementation details.
