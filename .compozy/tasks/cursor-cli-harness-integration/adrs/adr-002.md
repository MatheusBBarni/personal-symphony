# ADR-002: Stable First-Class Cursor Harness Product Posture

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-15

## Context

The approved idea already selected Cursor CLI as a bounded extension of Symphony's existing `Agent Harness` model.
The remaining product decision for the PRD is how strongly Symphony should position the first release.

The user wants the primary outcome to be clear provider choice for operators who already use Cursor CLI, wants Cursor
to be available to any `Logical Agent` role rather than only `engineer`, and wants the key success signal to be normal
task-flow completion on Cursor-selected roles. Those answers favor a stronger product posture than an experimental or
role-limited rollout.

At the same time, Cursor CLI is still documented as beta, and the surrounding idea work identified adoption risk,
provider drift, and safety expectations as real concerns. The PRD needs a product stance that is strong enough to
match user intent without turning one provider launch into an uncontrolled platform claim.

## Decision

The PRD will position Cursor as a stable first-class `Agent Harness` in Symphony's `Runtime Contract`.

This means the product story for V1 is not "advanced option," "beta integration," or "engineer-only pilot." Cursor is
presented as a supported Harness peer to existing Harnesses, and the product allows any `Logical Agent` role to select
it through the same `agents.<name>.harness` model used elsewhere.

Risk control will come from rollout structure and scope boundaries rather than weak product framing. The PRD will use a
phased rollout plan, conservative non-goals, and explicit readiness/visibility expectations to limit scope without
downgrading support posture.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Stable Engineer-First Launch

- **Description**: Treat Cursor as fully supported, but only for the `engineer` role in the first product release.
- **Pros**: Reduces user-facing scope at launch and narrows early support cases.
- **Cons**: Conflicts with the chosen requirement that Cursor should be available to any `Logical Agent`; weakens the
  provider-neutral story.
- **Why rejected**: The product value is broader than one role, and the user explicitly chose all `Logical Agent`
  roles as the right first product shape.

### Alternative 2: Experimental Or Advanced-Option Posture

- **Description**: Ship Cursor as a compatibility path, hidden option, or explicitly experimental integration first.
- **Pros**: Lowers immediate support expectations and creates more room for provider churn.
- **Cons**: Undercuts adoption by existing Cursor operators, weakens the provider-choice story, and makes the product
  appear less confident in its own Harness model.
- **Why rejected**: The user selected a stable support posture and wants success measured by real task-flow completion,
  not cautious exposure.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Matches operator expectations for a real supported provider choice.
- Strengthens Symphony's claim that `Agent Harnesses` are a stable product concept, not one-off integrations.
- Preserves role flexibility by allowing any `Logical Agent` to select Cursor.

### Negative

- Raises the quality bar for readiness, documentation, and observability at launch.
- Leaves less room to hide behind experimental framing if Cursor behavior shifts.

### Risks

- **Support burden risk**: Stable framing increases user expectations. Mitigation: keep scope narrow, define clear
  non-goals, and require strong readiness and visibility signals.
- **Provider drift risk**: Cursor beta changes may challenge stable positioning. Mitigation: rely on documented
  behaviors, preserve raw-log fallbacks, and phase rollout carefully.
- **Over-generalization risk**: Teams may assume all provider semantics are equivalent. Mitigation: keep parity claims
  specific and document role of selected-Harness behavior explicitly.

## Implementation Notes

- The PRD should describe stable support in business terms, while still using phased rollout to manage adoption and
  expectation-setting.
- The PRD should avoid experimental framing language unless tied to a specific non-goal or open question rather than
  the overall product posture.
