# ADR-002: Focused Compozy Queue Shortcut

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-13

## Context

Personal Symphony already supports an **Ordered Queue** through `--queue`, and Compozy-backed runs already participate in that flow through canonical selectors such as `compozy:<slug>`. The approved idea for this workflow identified a repeated operator pain point: in a **Workspace Repository** that already uses the Compozy-backed **Issue Tracker**, a solo operator thinks in terms of `.compozy/tasks/<slug>/` names and wants to queue known **Compozy PRD Runs** directly.

The product question for the PRD is not whether to redesign selectors across the product. The question is how broad the first product step should be. A narrow scope preserves the current product contract and focuses on the high-frequency queue-entry workflow. A broader scope could smooth more selector surfaces, but it would expand the MVP away from the validated operator need.

## Decision

The MVP will adopt a focused queue shortcut approach.

Bare Compozy PRD Run slugs are a user-facing shortcut only for `--queue`, and only when `.symphony/settings.json` selects the Compozy-backed **Issue Tracker** with `tracker.kind = "compozy_tasks"`. The MVP will prioritize ad hoc local terminal use, shorter queue commands, strict bare-slug input for this flow, and guided mismatch errors when an operator uses bare slugs while another tracker is selected.

The PRD will treat this as a narrow ergonomics improvement for one existing flow, not as the start of a wider selector simplification project.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Transitional Queue Experience

- **Description**: Make bare slugs the preferred queue input while also accommodating mixed habits and migration expectations around canonical selector styles.
- **Pros**: Softer transition for operators already accustomed to `compozy:<slug>` and potentially less friction during adoption.
- **Cons**: Weakens the clarity of the MVP contract and blurs the primary usage pattern the PRD is supposed to optimize.
- **Why rejected**: The user selected a stricter MVP centered on shorter ad hoc queue commands, not on transition management.

### Alternative 2: Broader Selector Simplification

- **Description**: Treat bare slug support as the first phase of a wider selector simplification effort across multiple selector-based flows.
- **Pros**: Creates a larger product story and could produce broader long-term consistency.
- **Cons**: Expands scope, mixes multiple product questions together, and dilutes the immediate value hypothesis for `--queue`.
- **Why rejected**: The validated need is specific to `--queue`, and the MVP should remain tightly scoped to that operator flow.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The PRD can stay tightly focused on one high-frequency operator workflow.
- Success is easy to define: shorter ad hoc queue commands for known **Compozy PRD Runs**.
- Product messaging stays clear that this is a Compozy-tracker-specific queue shortcut, not a new global selector system.

### Negative

- Operators will not get the same simplification in other selector-based flows during MVP.
- The product will still have different selector expectations across different flows and tracker modes.

### Risks

- Users may expect the same bare-slug behavior outside `--queue`. Mitigation: document the MVP boundary explicitly in the PRD and user-facing docs.
- Guided mismatch errors may raise expectations for deeper selector ergonomics work. Mitigation: define broader selector simplification as future work rather than implicit MVP scope.

## Implementation Notes

- The PRD should emphasize the ad hoc local terminal flow as the primary persona journey.
- The MVP success signal is shorter queue commands, not a generalized selector overhaul.
- The PRD should preserve compatibility expectations for existing canonical Compozy selectors and non-Compozy trackers.
